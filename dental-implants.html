A missing tooth, replaced at the root.

An implant is the only replacement that goes into the bone the way a real root does, which is why the jaw underneath stays where it is. Planned on a 3D scan and placed here by Dr. Silver, not referred across town.

Why the root is the part that matters

When a tooth comes out, the bone that held it starts to shrink, because nothing is loading it any more. Over a few years that shows up as a dip in the gum line, a change in the shape of the face around the mouth, and neighbouring teeth drifting into the gap and tilting.

A bridge covers the gap. A denture sits on top of it. Only an implant puts something back into the bone, and that is what keeps the bone.

How it goes here

1. Assessment and 3D scan

We take a cone beam scan so we can see the bone volume, the nerve position and the sinus in three dimensions. That is what tells us whether an implant will work, whether you need a graft first, and exactly where it should sit.

2. Grafting, if you need it

If there is not enough bone, Dr. Silver places a graft to rebuild it, including sinus lifts in the upper back jaw. This adds months to the timeline, and we will say so up front rather than discovering it halfway through.

3. Placement

The implant goes in under local anaesthetic, with IV sedation if you would rather not be present for it. Most people are back at work the next day.

4. Integration

The bone grows onto the implant surface over several months. In some cases a temporary tooth can go on much sooner, which is what teeth in a day refers to. Dr. Silver has trained specifically in that approach.

5. The crown

An iTero scan rather than an impression tray, then a crown made to match the teeth either side. It should be the part nobody notices.

One appointment to find out. Bring the question, get the scan, and leave with a real timeline and a real number. There is no obligation attached to it.

What else we will check

Implants fail for reasons that are usually visible in advance: untreated gum disease, heavy clenching that will overload the implant, uncontrolled diabetes, smoking. Because we run a full assessment anyway, those come out early rather than two years later. If something makes you a poor candidate we will tell you before you have spent anything.

Good to know

Implants, answered

How long does the whole process take?
It varies with the bone and the tooth. A straightforward case is often a placement appointment, then several months while the implant integrates with the bone, then the crown. Some cases allow a temporary tooth much sooner. We will give you the actual timeline for your mouth after the scan, not an average.
Does it hurt?
Placement is done under local anaesthetic and most patients report less discomfort afterwards than they expected, usually comparable to an extraction. If the idea of the appointment is the problem rather than the sensation, Dr. Silver is certified in IV sedation and you can sleep through it.
Am I a candidate?
You need enough bone to hold the implant and healthy gums around it. If the bone is not there, a graft can often build it back, which Dr. Silver also does here. Smoking, uncontrolled diabetes and untreated gum disease all affect the odds, and we will be straight with you about that rather than take your money and hope.
What does it cost?
It depends on how many implants, whether you need a graft or a sinus lift, and what goes on top. We give you a written breakdown with the treatment codes on it before anything starts, so you can check it against your insurance.
Is a bridge or a denture better?
Sometimes, yes. A bridge is quicker and cheaper but it means cutting down the healthy teeth either side. A denture is cheaper still but it sits on the gum and the bone underneath keeps shrinking. An implant is the only option that replaces the root, which is why the bone stays. We will lay out all three honestly for your situation.
